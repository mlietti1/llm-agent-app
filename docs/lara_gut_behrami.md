# Lara Gut-Behrami

> **Lara Gut-Behrami** (born Lara Gut) is a retired Swiss alpine ski racer, celebrated for her exceptional results in super-G, downhill, and giant slalom. She finished her career with 48 World Cup victories, two overall World Cup titles, three Olympic medals, and nine World Championship medals.

## At a glance

- **Full name:** Lara Gut-Behrami
- **Born:** April 27, 1991
- **Birthplace:** Sorengo, Ticino, Switzerland
- **Nationality:** Swiss
- **Height:** 1.60 m (5 ft 3 in)
- **Sport:** Alpine skiing
- **Club:** Sporting Gottardo
- **World Cup debut:** December 28, 2007, aged 16
- **Specialties:** Super-G and downhill; also competed in giant slalom and combined
- **Spouse:** Valon Behrami, former Swiss international footballer
- **Status:** Retired; announced the end of her career on August 5, 2026

## Why she is notable

Gut-Behrami is one of the most successful Swiss alpine skiers of her generation. Her major achievements include:

- **48 World Cup race victories** and **101 World Cup podiums**.
- **Two overall World Cup titles**, in 2016 and 2024.
- **Six super-G discipline titles**, a record in the women's World Cup.
- An Olympic gold medal in super-G at the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics.
- **Three Olympic medals** in total: one gold and two bronze.
- **Nine World Championship medals**, including two gold medals won at the 2021 championships.
- The distinction of becoming the first woman to record at least ten World Cup wins in three different disciplines.

## Early life and beginnings

Gut-Behrami was born in Sorengo, in Switzerland's Italian-speaking canton of Ticino. Her father, Pauli Gut, is Swiss, and her mother, Gabriella Almici, was born in Lombardy, Italy.

She began competing in FIS races as a teenager. In 2007, she won a downhill silver medal at the Alpine Junior World Championships and became Swiss national super-G champion. She also finished second in the European Cup downhill standings that year.

She made her World Cup debut in giant slalom at Lienz, Austria, in December 2007. In December 2008, at age 17, she won her first World Cup race—a super-G in St. Moritz—and became the youngest winner of a World Cup super-G at the time.

## Career overview

### Early breakthrough and first major medals

At the 2009 World Championships in Val-d'Isère, France, Gut won silver medals in both downhill and super combined before her 18th birthday. A hip dislocation during training later that year forced her to miss the 2010 season and the Vancouver Olympics. She returned to competition in 2011 and continued to build a strong record.

In December 2012, she won her first World Cup downhill. At the 2014 Sochi Winter Olympics, she earned her first Olympic medal, a bronze in downhill. That season she also won the World Cup super-G discipline title and finished third overall.

### Injury and comeback

At the 2017 World Championships in St. Moritz, Gut won bronze in super-G before injuring her left knee, including the ACL and meniscus, during the combined event. The injury ended her season.

She later returned to winning form, taking a World Cup downhill victory at Crans-Montana in February 2020. Her 2020–21 season marked a major resurgence: she won four consecutive World Cup super-G races and claimed two world titles at Cortina d'Ampezzo.

### World Championship breakthrough in 2021

At the 2021 World Championships, Gut-Behrami won gold in both super-G and giant slalom, plus bronze in downhill. The giant slalom win was especially notable because she prevailed by just 0.02 seconds. She became the second Swiss woman to win two gold medals at a single Alpine World Championships.

### Olympic champion in 2022

At the Beijing 2022 Winter Olympics, Gut-Behrami won Olympic gold in super-G and bronze in giant slalom. These results made her an Olympic champion and brought her Olympic medal tally to three.

### Record-setting 2024 season

In 2023–24, she won her second overall World Cup title and captured both the giant slalom and super-G discipline titles. She secured eight World Cup race victories during the season, including four in giant slalom, underlining her versatility beyond the speed events.

In 2025, she won silver in the team combined event at the World Championships in Saalbach, partnering Wendy Holdener. She also secured her sixth super-G discipline title, extending her record.

### Retirement in 2026

Gut-Behrami initially announced that she planned to retire after the 2025–26 season. However, a knee injury sustained in training in late 2025 ruled her out of the remainder of the season and ended her hopes of competing at the 2026 Milano Cortina Winter Olympics.

On **August 5, 2026**, she announced that she was ending her career with immediate effect. Her retirement brought to a close nearly two decades of elite competition.

## Selected career milestones

- **2007:** Won junior World Championship downhill silver and became Swiss national super-G champion.
- **2007:** Made her World Cup debut at age 16.
- **2008:** Won her first World Cup race, a super-G in St. Moritz, at age 17.
- **2009:** Won two silver medals at the World Championships.
- **2014:** Won downhill bronze at the Sochi Olympics and the World Cup super-G title.
- **2016:** Won her first overall World Cup title.
- **2017:** Won World Championship super-G bronze before a serious knee injury ended her season.
- **2021:** Won two World Championship gold medals, in super-G and giant slalom.
- **2022:** Won Olympic super-G gold and giant slalom bronze in Beijing.
- **2024:** Won her second overall World Cup title, plus the super-G and giant slalom titles.
- **2025:** Won her sixth super-G discipline title and World Championship team combined silver.
- **2026:** Announced her retirement on August 5 after an injury prevented her from competing in the Olympic season.

## Personal life

Gut-Behrami married Swiss footballer Valon Behrami in July 2018. After their marriage, she competed under the surname Gut-Behrami.

She speaks Italian, German, French, and English, and also knows Spanish. She has been associated with the ski brand Rolex since 2009, and has used Head ski equipment during much of her later career.

## Legacy

Gut-Behrami's legacy is defined by sustained excellence across multiple alpine disciplines, particularly super-G. Her 48 World Cup wins, 101 podiums, two overall titles, six super-G globes, Olympic gold, and two world championship gold medals make her one of the outstanding alpine skiers of her era. Her return to the top after serious injuries and her success in both speed and technical events are central parts of her career story.
